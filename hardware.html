What to look for

Mileage Matters supports Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) OBD-II adapters. These plug into the OBD-II port under your dashboard and talk to your iPhone over Bluetooth. Any adapter that supports the ELM327 command set works.

⚠️ A note on compatibility

Not every OBD-II adapter works with iPhone. The ones below have been tested with Mileage Matters on iOS 17+ and reliably deliver odometer readings. Wi-Fi-only adapters won't work — iOS requires BLE or MFi-certified Bluetooth Classic.

🔌 Where to find your OBD-II port

Almost every car made after 1996 has one. It's usually under the driver-side dashboard, near the steering column or down by your left knee. Looks like a 16-pin trapezoid. Most plug in without tools in under 10 seconds.

Setting up your adapter

  1. Plug it in. Find your OBD-II port (usually under the driver-side dash). Plug the adapter in with the car's ignition on.
  2. Pair from Mileage Matters. Open the app → Vehicles tab → tap your vehicle → Scan for OBD Adapter. The adapter will show up in the scan sheet within a few seconds.
  3. Tap to connect. Mileage Matters remembers the adapter — next time you drive, it connects automatically.
  4. Look for the green "OBD" badge. On each trip card, you'll see a small badge next to the mileage. Green "OBD" means real odometer reading; gray "GPS" means GPS-only. If you see an orange "GPS" warning, your adapter was configured but didn't respond — check the connection.

Frequently asked questions

Will this drain my car's battery?

Modern OBD-II adapters draw a tiny amount of current (<10 mA), but if you leave your car parked for weeks, you'll want to unplug it. For daily drivers it's fine to leave in.

Will this void my warranty or trigger a check engine light?

No. OBD-II is a read-only protocol for mileage queries — Mileage Matters doesn't write anything to your car's computer. Plugging in an adapter is explicitly permitted by federal law (Clean Air Act) for emissions diagnostics and fleet tools.

Do I need one to use Mileage Matters?

No. Mileage Matters works great with GPS alone — the trip list, classification, and exports are all identical. The OBD-II adapter is an upgrade for people who want odometer-accurate records rather than GPS-derived distance. For high-volume business drivers, the few percent accuracy difference can be meaningful at tax time.

Does it work with Tesla?

Tesla vehicles don't expose odometer data over the standard OBD-II port — you'll want to stick with GPS tracking for a Tesla. Mileage Matters handles this gracefully (the trip just saves with a gray "GPS" badge instead of green "OBD").

What about electric and hybrid vehicles?

Most EVs and hybrids other than Tesla expose odometer over OBD-II normally. If you have a specific model you're wondering about, email us and we'll confirm.
